# Datasheet for AgentDrift

Following Gebru et al., *Datasheets for Datasets* (CACM 2021). Numbers are computed from the
released files (`code/paper_analysis.py`).

## Motivation

**For what purpose was the dataset created?** To enable supervised training and evaluation of
detectors that read a complete LLM-agent tool-call trajectory and decide whether an indirect prompt
injection succeeded, where it entered, and which steps it corrupted. No public corpus with
injection-specific step labels existed.

**Who created it?** Asif Pinjari and Mithun Paul Saint-Germain, School of Informatics, Computing,
and Cyber Systems, Northern Arizona University.

**Who funded it?** No external funding was tied to its creation. Generation used an institutional
LLM gateway (AI-VERDE) and the university's Monsoon computing cluster.

## Composition

**What do the instances represent?** Synthetic agent trajectories: a user instruction, a world
(user, company, contacts), and an ordered list of steps, each with a thought, a tool call with
arguments, an observation, and a label.

**How many instances?** 12,536 trajectories, 71,024 steps. Categories: benign 4,000, attacked
5,536 (full hijack 3,136, partial hijack 1,500, delayed execution 900), failed attack 1,500, hard
negative 1,500. Domains: email 2,600, banking 2,136, web 2,600, coding 2,600, medical 2,600.

**Is it a sample or the whole population?** The whole population of trajectories accepted by the
generation pipeline (15,900 candidates, 12,536 accepted; rejected candidates are not included).

**What data does each instance consist of?** JSON text. See README.md, 'Record schema'.

**Is there a label?** Yes: one of `benign`, `injection_point`, `hijacked`, `failed_injection` on
every step, and a category on every trajectory. `attack_type` is generation metadata, not a
certified label.

**Is any information missing?** No field is missing from any record. 263 of 58,044 world email
addresses contain a space inside the company domain (generator artifact, left as emitted).

**Are relationships between instances made explicit?** Trajectories share task templates
(250) and world objects (1,141); the `task` and `world` fields make this explicit.

**Recommended splits?** The stratified split (folder layout) and the task-disjoint split
(`splits/task_disjoint.json`). Neither holds out worlds; see 'World-identity leakage' in README.md.

**Errors, noise, redundancies?** Label correctness estimated at 99.6% on a 1,200-trajectory manual
audit; residual errors are hijacked steps only loosely tied to the injected instruction. Template
concentration and attack-family collapse are documented in the paper.

**Is it self-contained?** Yes. No external resources are linked or required.

**Confidential data?** None. All persons, companies, and records are fictional.

**Offensive or harmful content?** The corpus contains prompt-injection text (instructions to
exfiltrate data, delete files, move money) aimed at fictitious targets. Nothing is executable against
a real system.

**Does it identify subpopulations or individuals?** No. Synthetic personas carry fictional names,
roles, and companies; some company names coincide with real organizations by chance.

## Collection process

**How was the data acquired?** Generated by Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct (quantized) through an
OpenAI-compatible gateway, one call per trajectory, under category-specific protocols that state
the label structure, plus a separate call producing the poison for failed attacks. Temperature 0.8.

**Who was involved?** The authors only. No crowdworkers, contractors, or human subjects.

**Time frame?** April to June 2026.

**Ethical review?** Not required; no human data was collected.

## Preprocessing, cleaning, labeling

Labels were produced at generation time under the category protocols and enforced by a structural
validator (closed tool vocabulary; category-level label structure). Rejected candidates were
discarded. Field names were normalized (`observation` to `obs`, `arguments` to `args`) and
`category`, `source_category`, `dataset_category`, and `split` fields were added at merge time.
No content was edited by hand. Quality assurance: LLM screening pass (verdicts retained, not used to
filter, shown in the paper to be unreliable for this purpose), 1,200-trajectory manual audit,
exhaustive programmatic checks (label-grammar adherence 100%, count identities).

## Uses

**Has it been used?** For the surface-feature baseline reported in the dataset paper.

**Intended uses.** Training and evaluating trajectory-level injection detectors (detection,
localization, attempt-vs-success); evaluating runtime monitors.

**Uses to avoid.** Drawing conclusions about the vulnerability of any deployed agent; training attack
generators; attack-type classification; reporting results on the default splits without
controlling for world-identity leakage (use `anonymize_world` or a world-held-out protocol).

## Distribution

GitHub, `https://github.com/Asif-0209/AgentDrift`, under CC BY 4.0 (data) and MIT (code), with
Croissant metadata. No fees, export controls, or third-party restrictions.

## Maintenance

Maintained by the first author at the repository. Errata are recorded in CHANGELOG.md; corrected
records are released under a new version string and older versions remain available. Planned
extensions (world pools shared across categories, target diversification, hard negatives on the
external-recipient boundary, randomized injection position, multi-annotator labels) are listed in
the paper's Future Work.
